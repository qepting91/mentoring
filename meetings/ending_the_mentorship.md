# Ending the Mentorship

*A guide for gracefully concluding your mentorship relationship.*

---

## The Sunset Clause in Action

From *One Minute Mentoring*: All mentorships should have defined endpoints. This isn't failure - it's intentional design.

**Reasons mentorships end:**
- Sunset date reached (as agreed in the Relationship Agreement)
- Goals achieved
- Circumstances changed (job, location, priorities)
- Natural evolution - mentee has outgrown this stage
- Mutual agreement that the relationship has run its course

- [ ] Update LinkedIn to reflect the relationship (if appropriate)
- [ ] Archive mentorship documents for future reference
- [ ] Schedule the agreed-upon follow-up (if any)

## If the Ending Is Difficult

Sometimes mentorships end before the sunset date for challenging reasons:

### If You're the Mentee Ending Early

**Be honest but professional:**

> "I've been reflecting on our mentorship, and I think it's time for me to conclude it. I'm grateful for [specific things], and I feel ready to [move on / seek a different type of support / etc.]."

**You don't owe a detailed explanation, but you do owe:**
- Honesty that you're ending it
- Gratitude for their time
- A respectful close

### If You're the Mentor Ending Early

**Be direct but kind:**

> "I've been thinking about our mentorship, and I believe we've reached a natural conclusion. You've made great progress on [specific areas], and I think you're ready for [next step]."

**Consider:**
- Is there someone else who'd be a better fit?
- Can you make an introduction?
- What's the kindest way to frame this?

## The Circle Continues

From *Lean In*: The best way to honor a mentorship is to become a mentor yourself.

**Mentee:** Consider how you can mentor someone else, even informally.

**Mentor:** Reflect on what this mentee taught you about mentoring.
