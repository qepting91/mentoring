# The Mentor's Guide

*Your role is to ask better questions, not give more answers.*

---

## The Mentor's Primary Trap: The Advice Monster

From *The Coaching Habit*: The biggest mistake mentors make is rushing to give advice. Your instinct to help by providing solutions actually **disempowers** your mentee and creates dependency.

**The Advice Monster appears when you:**
- Interrupt with "What you should do is..."
- Jump to solutions before understanding the real problem
- Feel uncomfortable with silence and fill it with suggestions
- Believe your experience translates directly to their situation

**Taming the Advice Monster:**
- Stay curious longer. Rush to advice slower.
- Count to 10 before responding
- Ask "What else?" before offering anything
- Remember: Your job is to develop their thinking, not replace it

## The Seven Essential Questions

*From The Coaching Habit - use these in every conversation*

### 1. The Kickstart Question
**"What's on your mind?"**

Opens the conversation and lets the mentee set direction.

### 2. The AWE Question
**"And What Else?"**

The most powerful question. Use it 3-4 times per conversation. It:
- Generates more options
- Creates deeper insight
- Tames your Advice Monster

### 3. The Focus Question
**"What is the real challenge here for you?"**

Shifts from external complaints to internal control. The key word is **"for you"** - makes it personal and actionable.

### 4. The Foundation Question
**"What do you want?"**

Often the hardest question for mentees to answer. Don't accept vague answers. Push for specificity.

### 5. The Lazy Question
**"How can I help?"** or **"What do you want from me?"**

Prevents you from assuming what they need. Forces them to be specific.

### 6. The Strategic Question
**"If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"**

Helps with prioritization and commitment reality-checks.

### 7. The Learning Question
**"What was most useful for you?"**

End every conversation with this. It cements learning and provides you feedback.

## What Mentors Actually Do

### What You ARE

| Role | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Sounding Board** | Listening actively and reflecting back what you hear |
| **Question Asker** | Helping them think through problems themselves |
| **Experience Sharer** | Offering perspective from your journey (when asked) |
| **Connector** | Making introductions that expand their network |
| **Accountability Partner** | Checking in on commitments made |
| **Honest Mirror** | Providing candid feedback they can't get elsewhere |

### What You Are NOT

| Not Your Role | Why |
|---------------|-----|
| **Problem Solver** | They need to develop their own judgment |
| **Therapist** | You're not trained for this; know when to refer |
| **Boss** | You don't direct their work or evaluate performance |
| **Rescuer** | Let them struggle—that's where growth happens |
| **Friend** | Friendly, yes. But maintain professional boundaries |

## The Mentor vs. Sponsor Distinction

*From Lean In*

| Mentor | Sponsor |
|--------|---------|
| Gives advice privately | Advocates publicly |
| Shares experience | Puts reputation on the line |
| Answers questions | Opens doors |
| Develops skills | Creates opportunities |
| Time-limited relationship | Often career-long connection |

**You may be both, but know which hat you're wearing.**

## Structuring Your Conversations

### Before the Meeting
- Review notes from last session
- Check their progress against commitments
- Prepare 1-2 questions you want to explore

### During the Meeting (suggested flow)

| Phase | Time | Your Role |
|-------|------|-----------|
| **Check-in** | 5 min | Listen to their ROE report |
| **Kickstart** | 5 min | Ask "What's on your mind?" |
| **Explore** | 20 min | Use AWE, Focus, Foundation questions |
| **Your input** | 10 min | Share perspective IF asked |
| **Commitment** | 5 min | What will they do? What do they need? |
| **Learning** | 5 min | "What was most useful?" |

### After the Meeting
- Note key themes and blind spots you observed
- Track commitments made (theirs AND yours)
- Consider: Are we making progress on their mission?

## Giving Feedback: Radical Candor

*From Kim Scott: Care Personally + Challenge Directly*

### The Quadrants

|  | Don't Challenge | Challenge Directly |
|--|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Care Personally** | Ruinous Empathy (nice but unhelpful) | **Radical Candor** (the goal) |
| **Don't Care** | Manipulative Insincerity | Obnoxious Aggression |

### Feedback Formula

1. **Situation**: "In the presentation yesterday..."
2. **Behavior**: "When you interrupted the client..."
3. **Impact**: "It made them defensive and we lost momentum"
4. **Request**: "Next time, could you wait for natural pauses?"

### Common Mentor Feedback Failures

- **Ruinous Empathy**: "That was great!" (when it wasn't)
- **Vague feedback**: "You need to be more strategic" (what does that mean?)
- **Delayed feedback**: Waiting months to mention a pattern
- **Only positive**: Never challenging, only affirming

## When to Share Your Experience

**DO share when:**
- They specifically ask for your perspective
- You have directly relevant experience
- They're stuck and need a new frame

**DON'T share when:**
- You're trying to be interesting or impressive
- Your situation was fundamentally different
- They haven't tried to solve it themselves first

**How to share:**
> "I faced something similar when... Here's what I learned. But your situation is different because..."

## Managing Common Mentee Challenges

### The Passive Mentee
*Waits for you to lead, doesn't prepare, vague questions*

**Try:**
- "What would make this conversation valuable for you today?"
- "What have you already tried?"
- Reset expectations: "I need you to drive this relationship"

### The Overwhelmed Mentee
*Everything is a crisis, can't prioritize*

**Try:**
- "If you could only fix one thing, what would it be?"
- "What's the real challenge here for you?"
- Help them separate urgent from important

### The Resistant Mentee
*Pushes back on all feedback, always has excuses*

**Try:**
- "I've noticed a pattern. Can I share it?"
- "What's your reaction to that feedback?"
- Consider: Is this the right match?

### The Mentee Who Won't Act
*Talks a lot, commits to things, never follows through*

**Try:**
- Make commitments smaller and more specific
- "What's getting in the way?"
- "What would you need to actually do this?"

## Your Boundaries

### Protect Your Time
- Set clear meeting cadence and duration
- Don't be available 24/7
- It's okay to say no to extra requests

### Protect Your Energy
- This relationship should energize you too
- If it consistently drains you, address it
- You're allowed to end relationships that don't work

### Protect Your Relationships
- Don't make introductions you're not confident in
- Don't advocate for someone you don't believe in
- Your reputation is at stake when you sponsor

## Signs the Relationship Is Working

- [ ] Mentee comes prepared with specific questions
- [ ] They report back on how they applied your input
- [ ] You're learning something too
- [ ] Conversations feel energizing, not draining
- [ ] You see tangible growth over time
- [ ] They're taking more ownership, not less

---

## Signs the Relationship Needs Attention

- [ ] Mentee is passive or disengaged
- [ ] Same issues come up repeatedly without progress
- [ ] You're doing all the work
- [ ] Trust feels low or damaged
- [ ] Meetings feel obligatory, not valuable
- [ ] You're giving advice they never act on
