# Conflict Resolution in Mentorship

*A guide for addressing disagreements and challenges in the mentorship relationship.*

---

## Why Conflict Happens

Even healthy mentorship relationships experience friction. Common sources:

| Source | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Mismatched expectations | Mentee wants advice; mentor only asks questions |
| Communication gaps | Different response time expectations |
| Advice not followed | Mentor feels ignored; mentee felt advice didn't fit |
| Commitment imbalance | One party more invested than the other |
| Feedback resistance | Difficulty giving or receiving honest feedback |
| Boundary issues | Too personal, too professional, or unclear lines |
| Growth divergence | Mentee's needs have evolved beyond mentor's expertise |

---

## The Radical Candor Approach

From *Radical Candor*: Address issues by Caring Personally AND Challenging Directly.

**Avoid these traps:**

| Approach | Problem |
|----------|---------|
| Ruinous Empathy | Caring but not addressing the issue directly |
| Obnoxious Aggression | Being direct but not showing you care |
| Manipulative Insincerity | Neither caring nor being direct |

**The goal:** Have the hard conversation because you care about the relationship.

## Before Having "The Conversation"

### Step 1: Self-Reflection

Answer honestly:

| Question | Your Response |
|----------|---------------|
| What specifically is bothering me? | |
| Is this a pattern or a one-time thing? | |
| Have I clearly communicated my expectations? | |
| What's my role in this situation? | |
| What outcome do I want? | |
| Am I willing to hear their perspective? | |

### Step 2: Check Your Assumptions

| Assumption I'm Making | Alternative Explanation |
|-----------------------|------------------------|
| | |
| | |

### Step 3: Decide If It's Worth Addressing

**Address it if:**
- It's affecting the value of the mentorship
- It's a recurring pattern
- It's eroding trust
- You're feeling resentful

**Let it go if:**
- It's a minor, one-time thing
- You haven't clearly communicated the expectation
- The relationship is ending soon anyway
- It's about preference, not impact

## How to Have the Conversation

### Framework: SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact)

**Structure your feedback:**

1. **Situation**: When/where did this happen?
2. **Behavior**: What specifically did they do (observable, not interpreted)?
3. **Impact**: How did it affect you or the relationship?

**Example:**

> "In our last two meetings [situation], when I shared my challenge, you immediately gave advice without asking questions [behavior]. I felt like you weren't really hearing my situation before responding, which made me hesitant to share [impact]."

### Conversation Template

**Opening:**
> "I value our mentorship and want to address something that's been on my mind. Is now a good time?"

**Share your perspective (SBI):**
> "I've noticed [situation + behavior]. The impact on me has been [impact]."

**Invite their perspective:**
> "I'd like to understand your view. What's your experience been?"

**Find common ground:**
> "What can we both do differently going forward?"

**Agree on next steps:**
> "Let's try [specific change]. Can we check in on this in [timeframe]?"

## Common Conflict Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Mentor Gives Too Much Advice

**Mentee might say:**
> "I really appreciate your experience. Sometimes I need space to think things through myself before getting advice. Could we try having you ask me a few questions first before sharing solutions?"

**Request:** More questions, less immediate advice.

---

### Scenario 2: Mentee Doesn't Follow Through

**Mentor might say:**
> "I've noticed the last few commitments haven't been completed. I want to make sure I'm being helpful. Is something blocking you, or should we adjust what we're working on?"

**Explore:** Is the mentee overwhelmed? Are the actions not relevant? Is something else going on?

---

### Scenario 3: Meetings Keep Getting Rescheduled

**Either party might say:**
> "Our last three meetings have been rescheduled. I want to make sure we're both getting value from this. Should we revisit our meeting frequency or find a time that works better?"

**Address:** Is the commitment realistic? Is one party losing interest?

---

### Scenario 4: Feedback Feels Harsh

**Mentee might say:**
> "I appreciate your direct feedback. Sometimes the way it's delivered makes it hard for me to hear. Could we try [specific request, e.g., starting with what's working]?"

**Request:** Adjust delivery while keeping honesty.

---

### Scenario 5: Mentee Seems Disengaged

**Mentor might say:**
> "I've noticed you seem less engaged in our recent meetings. I want to make sure this is still valuable for you. What's going on?"

**Explore:** Has something changed? Does the mentee need something different?

---

### Scenario 6: Different Expectations About the Relationship

**Either party might say:**
> "I want to make sure we're aligned on what this mentorship is. My understanding was [expectation]. What's your understanding?"

**Revisit:** The Relationship Agreement. Update if needed.

## When the Conflict Can't Be Resolved

Sometimes, despite best efforts, the relationship isn't working.

### Signs It May Be Time to End

- [ ] Multiple conversations haven't led to change
- [ ] One or both parties dread meetings
- [ ] Trust has been significantly damaged
- [ ] Goals have diverged too much
- [ ] The conflict is about fundamental values

### How to End Gracefully

See: **meetings/ending_the_mentorship.md**

**Key points:**
- Be honest but not brutal
- Express genuine gratitude for what worked
- Don't burn bridges
- Learn from it for future mentorships

## Repair Checklist

After a difficult conversation:

**Immediately:**
- [ ] Thank them for engaging in the conversation
- [ ] Summarize what you both agreed to
- [ ] Set a check-in date

**Within one week:**
- [ ] Follow through on your commitments
- [ ] Notice if things are improving

**At check-in:**
- [ ] Discuss: Is this working better?
- [ ] Adjust if needed
- [ ] Acknowledge progress

---

## Prevention: Building a Conflict-Resilient Relationship

**From the start:**
- [ ] Complete the Relationship Agreement with clear expectations
- [ ] Discuss communication preferences explicitly
- [ ] Agree on how you'll handle disagreements
- [ ] Build in regular "health checks" (see relationship_health_check.md)

**Ongoing:**
- [ ] Give feedback early, before issues become patterns
- [ ] Ask "What was most useful?" to surface unspoken concerns
- [ ] Regularly revisit: "Is this working for both of us?"
