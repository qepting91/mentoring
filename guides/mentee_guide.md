# The Mentee's Guide

*You own this relationship. Your mentor's job is to help, not to drive.*

---

## The Mentee's Primary Trap: Passive Waiting

From *One Minute Mentoring* and *Lean In*: The biggest mistake mentees make is waiting for their mentor to lead, guide, and fix things. This creates an unhealthy dependency and wastes everyone's time.

**Passive waiting looks like:**
- Showing up without an agenda
- Asking "What should I do?"
- Waiting to be told what to work on
- Expecting your mentor to drive the conversation
- Not following up on advice given

**Active ownership looks like:**
- Preparing specific questions before each meeting
- Reporting back on how you applied previous guidance
- Driving the agenda and scheduling
- Coming with problems you've already tried to solve
- Treating your mentor's time as precious

---

## The "Will You Be My Mentor?" Problem

**Bad approach:**
> "Will you be my mentor?"

This puts all the burden on them, is vague, and creates pressure.

**Better approach:**
> "I'm working on [specific challenge]. You've [specific relevant experience]. Could I get 30 minutes of your time to get your perspective on [specific question]?"

Then, AFTER that conversation, IF it was valuable:
> "That was incredibly helpful. Would you be open to meeting [frequency] to continue these conversations?"

**Key principle**: Start with a specific ask, prove you're worth the investment, then formalize.

## Your Responsibilities

### Before Every Meeting

- [ ] Send agenda 24 hours ahead
- [ ] Prepare your ROE report (Return on Energy)
- [ ] Identify 1-2 specific challenges to discuss
- [ ] Know what you want from this conversation

### During Every Meeting

- [ ] Start by sharing what you did with previous advice (ROE)
- [ ] State what you want to discuss today
- [ ] Be specific about what kind of help you need
- [ ] Take notes
- [ ] Commit to specific next steps

### After Every Meeting

- [ ] Send thank you with summary of commitments
- [ ] Actually do what you committed to
- [ ] Prepare to report back next time

## Return on Energy (ROE)

*From One Minute Mentoring: This is your most important responsibility.*

Your mentor invests time and energy in you. The "return" they get is seeing you **apply their guidance and grow**. If you don't close the loop, you're not providing ROE.

### The ROE Report

Every meeting should start with:

> "Last time you suggested I [X]. Here's what I did: [specific action]. Here's what happened: [result]. Here's what I learned: [insight]."

Even if you didn't follow the advice:

> "You suggested [X]. I decided to try [Y] instead because [reason]. Here's what happened..."

**This shows:**
- You took their input seriously
- You're taking action
- You're learning
- Their time is valued

## How to Ask Good Questions

### Bad Questions (Don't ask these)

| Bad Question | Why It's Bad |
|--------------|--------------|
| "What should I do?" | You haven't done the thinking |
| "How do I get promoted?" | Too vague to be actionable |
| "Can you help me?" | Help how? With what? |
| "What's your advice?" | For what specifically? |

### Good Questions (Ask these instead)

| Good Question | Why It Works |
|---------------|--------------|
| "I'm facing [specific situation]. I've tried [A] and [B]. What am I missing?" | Shows you've done work |
| "You successfully [X]. What did you learn from that experience?" | Asks for specific wisdom |
| "I'm deciding between [Option A] and [Option B]. What would you consider?" | Specific and bounded |
| "I notice I tend to [pattern]. Do you see this? What's your perspective?" | Shows self-awareness |

### The Specificity Rule

**Vague**: "How do I become a better leader?"

**Specific**: "I have trouble giving critical feedback to my team. When you've had to deliver difficult messages, how did you approach it?"

## What You Can Ask Your Mentor For

### Appropriate Asks

- [ ] Perspective on a specific challenge
- [ ] Feedback on your approach or work
- [ ] Introduction to someone specific (with clear reason why)
- [ ] Accountability check-ins
- [ ] Their experience with a similar situation
- [ ] Help thinking through a decision
- [ ] Honest assessment of your blind spots

### Inappropriate Asks

- [ ] Doing your work for you
- [ ] Making decisions for you
- [ ] Solving all your problems
- [ ] Being available 24/7
- [ ] Guaranteeing outcomes
- [ ] Personal therapy or counseling
- [ ] Advocating for you before they trust you

## Sitting at the Table

*From Lean In*

### Don't Wait to Be Invited

- Schedule the meetings yourself
- Propose the agenda
- Ask for what you need directly
- Advocate for yourself

### Don't Apologize for Existing

Avoid:
- "Sorry to bother you..."
- "This is probably a dumb question..."
- "I don't want to take too much of your time..."

Instead:
- "I'd like your perspective on..."
- "I'm working through a challenge..."
- "I have a specific question about..."

### Take Up Space

- Come with opinions, not just questions
- Share what you think before asking what they think
- Disagree respectfully when you see it differently
- Own your accomplishments—don't minimize them

## The Sunset Clause

*From One Minute Mentoring*

Every mentorship should have a defined endpoint. This:
- Reduces pressure on both parties
- Creates urgency and focus
- Makes renewal a conscious choice
- Provides natural exit if needed

### How It Works

1. Set an initial end date (typically 3-6 months)
2. Schedule a midpoint check-in
3. At the end, explicitly decide: renew, evolve, or conclude
4. If renewing, set new goals and new end date

### Ending Gracefully

Not every mentorship lasts forever. It's okay—even healthy—to conclude when:
- You've achieved your goals
- Your needs have changed
- The relationship isn't working
- Your mentor can't give you what you need next

**End with gratitude**, not ghosting:
> "I've learned [X, Y, Z] from our work together. I think I'm ready to apply this on my own / I need [different kind of help] now. Thank you for investing in me."

## When Things Aren't Working

### If You're Not Getting Value

Before blaming your mentor, check yourself:
- Are you coming prepared?
- Are you being specific?
- Are you acting on their input?
- Have you told them what you actually need?

Then, if still not working:
> "I want to make sure we're getting value from our time together. I feel like [specific issue]. Can we talk about how to make this more effective?"

### If Your Mentor Gives Too Much Advice

Some mentors have their own "Advice Monster." You can redirect:
> "That's helpful. Before we go further, can I share what I've already tried?"
> "I'd love to think this through with you rather than get the answer. Can you help me explore the options?"

### If You Need to End It

Be direct, not ghosting:
> "I've appreciated your guidance on [X]. My focus is shifting to [Y], and I think I need a different kind of support. I wanted to tell you directly rather than just fade away."

## Building Your "Tribe of Mentors"

*From Tim Ferriss*

You don't need one perfect mentor. You need multiple perspectives:

| Mentor Type | What They Provide |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Industry mentor** | Technical/domain expertise |
| **Career mentor** | Navigation and advancement |
| **Peer mentor** | Shared experience and mutual support |
| **Reverse mentor** | Fresh perspectives, new skills |
| **Life mentor** | Values, balance, big picture |

### How to Build It

- Different mentors for different questions
- Some formal, some informal
- Books and podcasts as "mentors"
- People you've never met but learn from

## Your Pre-Meeting Checklist

Copy and use before every meeting:

```
□ ROE prepared (what I did with last advice + results)
□ Agenda sent 24+ hours ahead
□ 1-2 specific challenges identified
□ For each challenge:
  □ What have I already tried?
  □ What specifically do I want from my mentor?
□ Any commitments from last time—update ready
□ Questions written down (specific, not vague)
```

## Signs You're Being a Great Mentee

- [ ] Your mentor looks forward to meetings (they'll tell you)
- [ ] You're taking action between meetings
- [ ] You're growing—and you can articulate how
- [ ] You're driving the relationship, not waiting
- [ ] You come with problems you've already worked on
- [ ] You close the loop on advice given

---

## Signs You Need to Change Your Approach

- [ ] Your mentor seems checked out or rushed
- [ ] You leave meetings without clear next steps
- [ ] The same issues keep coming up
- [ ] You're waiting for your mentor to solve things
- [ ] You're not acting on guidance
- [ ] You can't articulate what you're learning
